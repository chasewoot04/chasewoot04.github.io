# Deltarune

## Observing Leitmotifs and Developing Themes

## An Introduction to Leitmotifs

Perhaps one of the most interesting and useful techniques used in soundtracks is the leitmotif--a musical technique where a composer establishes a melody associated with a character, theme, or circumstance, then reuses that melody in other places in a collection of music. Leitmotifs are particularly interesting because, in soundtracks--and especially video game soundtracks--they're often used for storytelling, and when used in this way, reveal new things about characters, foreshadow how plot points are related, or create a specific tone and/or atmosphere.

## Deltarune and Toby Fox

Toby Fox--creator of Undertale and Deltarune--is notorious for intricately using leitmotifs, and his music is a perfect example of how leitmotifs can be interpreted to gain deeper meaning of a piece of media's plot or themes. In Deltarune's soundtrack, you can see Fox play with motifs, and it is especially worthy of analysis due to the soundtrack's insistence on theming and mystery--both large focuses in the game and poignant due to its chapter-based release schedule. In this page, we'll focus on one instance of leitmotif use, and how it informs Deltarune's story: the Sanctuaries of Chapter 4

## The Sanctuaries

![One of Deltarune's Dark Fountains](https://media.tenor.com/OzLBMmUdbdMAAAAe/deltarune-dark-fountain.png)

"The Sanctuaries" refers to three distinct fantasy worlds--or "Dark Worlds"--that the player and their companions visit while the story progresses. Specifically, the moniker "Sanctuary" comes from the names of the musical tracks that play as the background music while the player is in these areas. These tracks are named "Dark Sanctuary," "The Second Sanctuary," and "The Third Sanctuary."

### Dark Sanctuary

Maybe one of the first things you notice when you hear "Dark Sanctuary" is its 6/8 time signature[^1]. All of the sanctuary songs focus on the eighth note, thought they progressively get more complex. Initially, the song uses a simple arpeggiated rhythm to support a main melody. Already, leitmotifs are layered in the main melody. Two motifs--"Don't Forget" and "The Legend"--make up the majority of the melody. "Don't Forget"--Deltarune's main theme--grounds the track in heroic tension and "The Legend" connects the song to the mysterious prophecy stated at the beginning of the game when that track plays. "Dark Sanctuary" mostly remixes these two songs, and its only unique leitmotif is the background arpeggio, which is expanded on later.

![Screenshot of the Prophecy Panels in the First Sanctuary](https://preview.redd.it/chapter-4-full-prophecy-in-continuity-at-a-guess-v0-nm1kziaxyx6f1.png?width=1020&format=png&auto=webp&s=637fefdfed75a4016b564eacb161668689359028)

What we can take from this opening track, then, is that this area is meant to be one where Deltarune's "prophecy" is at the forefront of the player's mind, and excerpts are the prophecy are scattered throughout the area. This portion of the game marks Deltarune's midpoint, and the atmosphere is tense and climactic. This is the story's turning point.

### The Second Sanctuary

Here's where things get interesting.

![Susie Creating a Dark Fountain](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/87brUMxu0N4/maxresdefault.jpg)

"The Second Sanctuary" is shocking because it plays when one of the characters of the player's party--Susie--opens a second Dark World. Dark Worlds are considered dangerous, and a magic that is largely associated with villains. Susie's decision to open a second one, therefore, is meant to be foreboding and eerie.

"The Second Sanctuary" opens with a similar arpeggio as the first--an echo that resembles the first track. There is added space between echoes, and strings crescendos in the background, increasing tension. Then, the track reveals its true colors. The previous leitmotifs are heard again, but recontextualized for the story's tone and themes. Fox adapts the melody to slide along the musical throughline, using odd synths to slowly drag one pitch to the next without a break in-between.

Yet the most interesting part of this track is its time signatures (yes, plural!). The opening arpeggio is so disorienting because it is in 9/4[^2]--one past the typical expected beat. But after the intro, the time signature switches to 3/4[^3]. Finally, a 10/8 (or 5/4, depending on how you're counting) section appears, completely changing the feel of the song.

Through experimenting with the tone and organization of leitmotifs, then, and adding slightly adjusted meters, Toby Fox once again reiterates on the themes present within the Sanctuaries. The player is still on a prophetic journey, but Susie's influence on the new environment is tangible and *wrong* in a sense, breaking apart the music (and atmosphere) she created with her naivete.

### The Third Sanctuary

"The Third Sanctuary" is at the Climax of Deltarune Chapter 4. After closing Susie's fountain, the party must descend *even deeper* into the third Dark World of this Chapter. Not only does this indicate a descent into more secrets, more reveals relating to the prophecy, and a final confrontation with this chapter's villain--the Roaring Knight--but it continues to develop the leitmotifs that have been used throughout this chapter so far, and it continues the theme of the Dark Fountains' entropic nature, that will eventually culminate in the Roaring Knight making yet *another* fountain *inside of* a Dark World, creating a monster prophesied to bring the world closer to catastrophe.

![The Knight Creating The Titan](https://i.redd.it/could-titans-be-shaped-around-the-will-of-its-creator-just-v0-bwzxguh00qog1.jpg?width=1559&format=pjpg&auto=webp&s=5fa48eb2f98860e2b104706a7d9402ad9744fa7d)

"The Third Sanctuary" is very similar in structure to "The Second Sanctuary." There is still the arpeggiated 9/4 opening, the 10/8 (or 5/4) secondary sequence, and development of the same themes, but each of these techniques--repeated leitmotifs, odd time signatures, different synths, etc.--are intensified and once again augmented to reflect theme and atmosphere.

The differences are prevalent, when you look at them. The Third Sanctuary, being a deeper version of the last two tracks, relies heavily on the arpeggiated 8th notes that have been brought more and more to the forefront of the melodies and dominates the melody of this final song. In contrast, the "The Legend" melody is heard only once (and quietly) while the "Don't Forget" leitmotif is discarded entirely. The removal of the "Don't Forget" leitmotif is particularly shocking, seeming to suggest distance from the song's hopeful suggestion that "I'm with you in the dark."

But once again, as is the focus of these tracks, time signature is by far the most prevalent difference. The Third Sanctuary is the culmination of the Sanctuaries' development, and is famous for its odd and sometimes baffling time signatures. The main melody of the song is in 11/8, this time *removing* a beat from clean 4 or 3 beat organization. This--and the fact that the bass drum hits every 8th note--provides an energy to the track that continually moves it forward, with one measure effectively "falling" into the next. The time signature has additional moments of complexity when a 2/4 beat is added arbitrarily in the beginning, and ultimately in its conclusion when it launches into a mind-boggling 23/8 time signature. Each movement creates tension and forward motion, as the environment becomes more and more alien. It's building to a conclusion, and it leads beautifully into this Chapter's climax.

## Conclusion

Toby Fox's techniques create a cascading motion throughout the Sanctuaries that culminates in the Third Sanctuary and the climax of the Chapter. Through repetitive musical themes (leitmotifs), developing melodies, entropic organizations, and odd time signatures, he tells a story not only through plot but also through music that drives the atmosphere and tone of the Chapter towards its eventual conclusion. These advanced techniques, and how they're used masterfully throughout even this small portion of Deltarune's soundtrack, are an impressive case study on how leitmotifs can be changed and recontextualized to tell story. We can certainly learn from the Sanctuaries and the musical techniques of their themes. Leitmotifs are a powerful tool, and Deltarune, Toby Fox, and the Sanctuary themes show how they can be used to create amazing effects that complement the media they're in.

[^1]: That is, 6 eighth notes per measure. Most songs are in 4/4: 4 quarter notes per measure.
[^2]: 9 quarter notes per measure.
[^3]: This is different from 6/8 because the emphasis is placed on each quarter note, rather than each group of three eighth notes.
