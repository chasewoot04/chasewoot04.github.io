Chapter 1

“We live at the verge of Starfall, each human at the mercy of those burning, brilliant souls above—our silent, churning masters, whose whims decide the course of today, yesterday, and tomorrow."

- The last words of Meiyand the Theologian; moments before her suicide

He had tried to dissect a sun; and the prison walls were what he had to show for it.

Bader sulked in the corner of that rigid, grayscale room, its flat, practical walls like sheets of metal which sought to suffocate him. They were sleek, but hard, as characteristic of the prison as any other surface that lay in its caverns and corridors. Even the bench that he sat on was paradoxically smooth yet hard as stone, offering him no comfort in the dry, still air. Each plane was its own prison, each material a reflection of the pristine stiffness of the wardens and their attempts at confinement.

But Bader was no warden, no bureaucrat, no politician who would admire the efficiency and cleanliness of the steel walls. Instead, he was condemned to live in them. All for seeking something that others were too scared to see.

A fist thudded on the steel door, shocking him from his sulking. Pulling the slit at eye height open, a prison guard—Hadar, Bader thought he was called—shouted at him through the thick slab. “Visitor for you, Starstealer,” he said callously. “Best finish up your heresy in there before we open up.”

Bader glared at the man through the slit, not responding to the insult but striving to convey with his eyes that he could tear the man apart in seconds were he to be completely free. Starstealer. What a shining joke. He'd hoped that the influence of the Zoros hadn't reached far enough from their idyllic moon to influence the wardens who administered this place, but, of course, he was mistaken for having any expectations of the sort.

“Heresy is merely a term the unenlightened use for things they don't yet understand,” Bader said, poorly veiling the malice in his voice. “I'd curb your accusations before I've actually had a trial, you buffoon.”

“If there weren't cameras in this place, I'd be inclined to teach you a lesson, speaking like that to your betters.”

“If there weren't cameras in here, I'd be saying much worse,” Bader said, standing and stretching as much as he could in the cramped space. “But alas, my words have the tendency to be used against me, good sir. Such is the behavior of the 'righteous' governments that persecute me.”

Hadar snorted. “Justice would seem like persecution to someone like you, wouldn't it?”

Bader held his gaze, waiting for the door to unlock.

“Bah!” Hadar shouted. “It'll be over soon enough.” He pulled out a phone from his pocket and tapped it against the door, the electronic lock turning green before unlatching. “It won't disappoint me to see your body incinerated by the very sun you thought to desecrate.”

The door opened inwards, and Bader stepped away, avoiding the metal which threatened to pulverize his face. As much as anyone else in his situation might desire death, Bader wanted to live, thank you very much. He moved in front of the guard when the door stopped and offered his wrists to the man, smiling subserviently.

Impossibly, Hadar's scowl deepened even more as he locked cuffs onto Bader's fingers, tapping his phone against them to secure the smooth, black metal. “Sun's setting soon, Starstealer,” he said, “I want no funny business when the windows open, you hear me?”

Bader held up his hands. “Can't do much with my hands literally tied, can I?”

Hadar grabbed the bar between Bader's hands and yanked it towards the floor, painfully pulling his back muscles as the man's lanky arms ripped his short ones downward. He put his foul-smelling face to close to his and let out in a harsh whisper “I know exactly what you're capable of, you piece of scum. I know why the windows close when the sun comes out. I know why we bind your hands in the first place. So, once again, I tell you: no funny business when the windows open.”

Bader squinted at Hadar, wishing beyond anything that he could spit another snarky comment at him, but reading the large man's face told him that he'd gone far enough. A man could only get into so many precarious situations before he understood when and when not to provoke others. So Bader stood silent, letting the heat of the enclosed corridors wash over his muscles as Hadar slowly released the tension on his cuffs.

“Let's go,” was all the guard said, before they set off through the bowels of the fortress prison, the hum of the machines that kept the doors locked tight bouncing off the walls.

The corridors were as plain as Bader's cell, still paved in metallic grays, but there was a hexagonal shape to them that denied the harsh right angles of the prison. The walls bowed slightly outward to a vertical plane, which bowed inward once again to meet the ceiling, blessedly allowing those not locked in cells to feel some sort of relief from the claustrophobia that one almost constantly felt when walking through the fortress.

He'd been warned against it—harshly, in fact, but Bader turned his head to the walls, where the vertical plane at his eye height sported a row of locked windows. He followed Hadar closely, trying his best not to arouse suspicion. Just this once, he would be complacent, so long as he could glimpse the view once more. His fingertips tingled on his hands.

An alarm blared from the prison's speakers as, sure enough, the windows hissed, the metal panels which had sealed them shut sliding open to reveal the world outdoors.

Bader gazed past the glass—into the molten landscape that lay outside of the prison walls. Massive continents of rock floated on that surface of magma, constantly drifting slowly, leisurely, across the seas of fire that supported them. Every once in a while, the chasms that separated the sheets of rock would spit up embers that died sputtering on the basalt, cooling as their red glows faded. Beyond those rocks, Bader caught a glimpse of a beautiful sight, before it faded below the horizon.

Taemon had virtually no atmosphere, the molten planet which housed the fortress prison held together primarily by the force of gravity and the care of its visitors to remember not to walk out and expose themselves to the elements. So it was that when Bader looked upon the fading sun, there was no brilliant sunset, like his home planet now far away. There was only the bubbling wrath of the massive red star whose semicircle lethargically disappeared, its last rays shining upon him.

He stopped in the middle of the hallway, feeling the breath of the star on his face. For just an instant, as it faded from view, he basked in the sunlight, feeling a tug in his chest that pulled him towards the giant ball of gas thousands of miles away. A chill ran down his spine—a shock of energy starting from his neck and traveling through his entire nervous system, flooding his head, fingers, and toes.

It was ruined when Hadar yanked on his cuffs, starting him forward down the hallway and pulling him away from his indulgences. The scruffy guard bore his eyes into Bader's, at once conveying both disgust and derision as he hastened down the hallway, boots echoing on the floor. Sighing, Bader followed, quickening his pace even as he felt the influence of the stars disappear from him, leaving him in growing, pitiful darkness.
