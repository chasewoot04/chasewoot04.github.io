## An Introduction to Leitmotifs

Perhaps one of the most interesting and useful techniques used in soundtracks is the leitmotif. While not independent to soundtracks, the leitmotif is a musical technique heavily associated with them, because it gives the composer the ability to link together discrete themes, characters, or associations by calling back to a previously used melody.

Simply put, a leitmotif is a musical technique where a composer establishes a melody with a particular character, theme, or circumstance, then reuses that melody in other places in a piece or collection of music to connect it to that previous melody. Leitmotifs are particularly interesting because, in soundtracks--and especially video game soundtracks--they're often used for storytelling, and when used in this way, reveal new things about characters, foreshadow how plot points are related, or create a specific tone and/or atmosphere.

## Deltarune and Toby Fox

Toby Fox--creator of the video games Undertale and Deltarune--is notorious for his use of leitmotifs, and his music is a perfect example of how identifying leitmotif melodies and seeing them throughout a soundtrack can aid the listener's interpretation of it. In Undertale, Toby Fox's first work, every song is connected in some way, creating a masterpiece of interwoven tracks that all point to each other, whether it be thematically or to link the characters or world together.

But Deltarune is unique in the fact that it builds on Fox's previous work and methods with leitmotifs. You can see him play with them in Deltarune's soundtrack, and it is especially worthy of analysis due to the soundtrack's insistence on theming and mystery--both large focuses in the game and poignant due to its chapter-based release schedule. In this page, we'll focus on two instances of leitmotif uses, and how they inform Deltarune's story: the Sanctuaries of Chapter 4, and the character of the Roaring Knight.

## The Sanctuaries

![One of Deltarune's Dark Fountains](https://media.tenor.com/OzLBMmUdbdMAAAAe/deltarune-dark-fountain.png)

"The Sanctuaries" is a name that refers to a particular moment in Chapter 4 of Deltarune where the player and their companions explore three distinct fantasy worlds--or "Dark Worlds"--while the story progresses. Specifically, the moniker "Sanctuary" comes from the names of the musical track s that play as the background music for the majority of the time the player spends in these areas. Because of the nature of having multiple Sanctuaries, the tracks are aptly named "Dark Sanctuary," "The Second Sanctuary," and "The Third Sanctuary."

### Dark Sanctuary

Maybe one of the first things you notice when you hear "Dark Sanctuary" is its time signature. The song is in the meter 6/8, which roughly translates to 6 beats within a measure, focused on the eighth note[^1]. This is a trend that will continue across the sanctuaries as Toby Fox continues to experiment with measures. Initially, the song feels fairly simple, using an arpeggiated background rhythm to support a main melody. Already, leitmotifs are layered in the main melody. The main theme of Deltarune--"Don't Forget"--is revisited to ground the track in heroic tension and a sense of adventure. Further references are to a track called "The Legend"--which calls back to the mysterious prophecy established at the beginning of the game and continually running through Deltarune's narrative. Interestingly enough, the main melody of Dark Sanctuary is almost entirely defined by these two leitmotifs. It is more of a remix than an independent song to itself, and the only leitmotif it independently defines is in the background, and is reused in the subsequent themes.

![Screenshot of the Prophecy Panels in the First Sanctuary](https://preview.redd.it/chapter-4-full-prophecy-in-continuity-at-a-guess-v0-nm1kziaxyx6f1.png?width=1020&format=png&auto=webp&s=637fefdfed75a4016b564eacb161668689359028)

What we can take from this experimentation, where themes from the beginning of the game are reiterated, prophetic messages recalled, and heroics solidified, is that this area is meant to be one where Deltarune's "prophecy" is at the forefront of the player's mind. And indeed, this portion of the game marks Deltarune's midpoint, and where the story begins to find its center. In fact, in this area, you'll find scattered excerpts from the prophecy, further drawing you in.

[^1]: Most songs are in 4/4, which defines 4 quarter notes per measure.
